# Daily Kitchen Checklist Template for Small Restaurants

Running a small restaurant or café means dozens of small tasks need to happen every day.

Some are obvious: turn on equipment, check refrigerators, prepare ingredients, clean workstations, receive deliveries, restock, and close down safely.

Others are easy to forget when service gets busy.

That is why a simple **daily kitchen checklist** can be one of the most useful operational tools in a small restaurant.

The goal is not to create paperwork. The goal is to make sure the important work gets done without relying on one person to remember everything.

## What Is a Kitchen Checklist?

A kitchen checklist is a repeatable list of operational tasks that should be completed during a shift or at a specific time of day.

It can be organized around:

- opening
- preparation
- service
- cleaning
- stock
- closing
- safety
- maintenance

A checklist can be printed, kept in a spreadsheet, or managed digitally.

For small teams, the best checklist is usually the simplest one the staff will actually follow.

## Simple Daily Kitchen Checklist Template

### Opening Checklist

- Unlock and inspect kitchen
- Turn on required equipment
- Check refrigerator and freezer temperatures
- Inspect prep areas
- Check handwashing stations
- Confirm cleaning supplies are available
- Review staff assignments
- Check low-stock items
- Review deliveries expected today
- Begin prep list

### Prep Checklist

- Prepare ingredients
- Label and date prepared items
- Rotate stock using FIFO
- Refill stations
- Check portioning
- Confirm allergen and special-prep needs
- Update missing ingredients
- Record waste created during prep

### During Service

- Keep prep stations clean
- Refill low items
- Monitor stock levels
- Record waste
- Check incoming deliveries
- Complete scheduled cleaning tasks
- Communicate shortages to staff
- Assign urgent tasks when needed

### Closing Checklist

- Store remaining food correctly
- Label and date open items
- Record waste and leftovers
- Update low-stock items
- Clean prep surfaces
- Clean equipment
- Sweep and mop floors
- Empty waste bins
- Check refrigerators and freezers
- Turn off non-essential equipment
- Confirm tomorrow's prep needs
- Complete final safety check

This list should be adapted to the restaurant. A bakery, coffee shop, food truck, and full-service restaurant will not need the exact same tasks.

## Why Opening and Closing Checklists Matter

Opening and closing are two moments when operational mistakes are easiest to prevent.

A good opening checklist helps the team start service ready.

A good closing checklist helps make sure the kitchen is clean, stocked, and prepared for the next day.

Without a checklist, important tasks often depend on memory. That becomes risky when a new employee is working, the manager is absent, the restaurant is busy, several people share responsibility, or the team changes frequently.

## Assign Tasks to Specific People

A checklist becomes more useful when every task has an owner.

Instead of:

> Clean fridge

use:

> Clean walk-in fridge — assigned to Marcus — due before 22:30

Useful task information can include:

- task name
- assigned employee
- due time
- status
- priority
- notes
- recurring frequency

This creates accountability without requiring constant verbal follow-up.

## Use Recurring Tasks for Repeated Work

Many kitchen tasks happen every day or every week.

### Daily

- opening check
- closing clean
- temperature checks
- waste logging
- low-stock review
- prep-station cleaning

### Weekly

- deep-clean refrigerator
- check supplier order list
- inspect dry storage
- clean extraction filters
- review recurring waste
- complete full stock count

### Monthly

- equipment inspection
- inventory review
- supplier review
- storage organization
- maintenance checks

Recurring tasks reduce the need to recreate the same checklist every day.

## Keep the Checklist Short Enough to Use

A common mistake is turning a checklist into a huge operations manual.

If every shift has 80 tasks, staff may stop using it.

Start with the tasks that genuinely matter.

Ask:

- What must happen every day?
- What causes problems when forgotten?
- What affects food safety?
- What affects tomorrow's service?
- What does the manager repeatedly remind people to do?

Those tasks belong on the checklist first.

## Paper Checklist vs Kitchen Checklist App

Paper is a reasonable place to start. It is cheap, visible, and simple.

But paper has limitations:

- no reminders
- no history
- difficult to assign tasks
- easy to lose
- no automatic recurring tasks
- managers cannot easily see what is incomplete

A kitchen checklist app becomes more useful when the team needs recurring tasks, staff assignments, completion history, multiple shifts, multiple devices, or manager visibility.

The important thing is not whether the checklist is digital. The important thing is whether it improves the operation.

## Kitchen Checklist FAQ

### What should be on a restaurant opening checklist?

Typical items include equipment checks, temperatures, prep stations, stock levels, staff assignments, cleaning, and expected deliveries.

### What should be on a restaurant closing checklist?

Common closing tasks include food storage, labeling, cleaning, waste logging, stock checks, equipment shutdown, and preparation for the next day.

### How long should a kitchen checklist be?

There is no perfect number. Keep it short enough that staff will actually use it and detailed enough that important tasks are not forgotten.

### Should every task be assigned to a person?

Important tasks should usually have a clear owner. Shared responsibility often leads to tasks being forgotten.

### Is a kitchen checklist app better than paper?

Not always. Paper is fine for very small teams. An app becomes more useful when you need recurring tasks, assignments, history, reminders, or manager visibility.

## Conclusion

A good kitchen checklist does not make the team slower. It removes uncertainty.

Everyone knows what needs to happen, who is responsible, when it should be finished, and what is still incomplete.

Start with opening and closing tasks. Keep the list simple. Assign responsibility clearly. Then improve it as you learn where your kitchen actually needs more structure.
