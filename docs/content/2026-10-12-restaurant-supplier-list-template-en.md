# Restaurant Supplier List Template: What to Track, Review & Update

A restaurant may work with only a few suppliers at the beginning.

Then the list grows.

Produce comes from one company. Meat comes from another. Coffee, packaging, cleaning products, beverages, bakery items, and emergency purchases may all come from different places.

Soon, important supplier information is spread across:

- invoices
- email
- WhatsApp
- text messages
- spreadsheets
- paper notes
- staff memory

A simple **restaurant supplier list** solves a surprisingly large part of this problem.

It gives the team one place to see:

- who supplies each product
- who to contact
- what day to order
- when deliveries normally arrive
- current or recent prices
- minimum order requirements
- payment terms
- backup suppliers
- supplier issues that need follow-up

You do not need a complicated procurement system to start.

A clear supplier list is enough.

## Restaurant Supplier List Template

Here is a simple structure you can copy into a spreadsheet, document, or supplier-management app.

| Supplier | Category | Main Products | Contact | Order Day | Delivery Day | Minimum Order | Payment Terms | Last Price Check | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Green Valley Produce | Produce | Tomatoes, lettuce, onions | orders@example.com | Mon / Thu | Tue / Fri | 1,500 SEK | 14 days | 8 Oct | Usually reliable |
| North Foods | Meat | Chicken, beef | +46 XX XXX XX XX | Tue | Wed | 2,500 SEK | 30 days | 6 Oct | Confirm substitutions |
| City Roasters | Coffee | Espresso beans | sales@example.com | Fri | Mon | 10 kg | Card | 4 Oct | Backup blend available |
| Pack & Go | Packaging | Cups, lids, takeaway boxes | portal order | Wed | Fri | 1 carton | Invoice | 1 Oct | Check lid compatibility |

The exact columns can change depending on your restaurant.

The important thing is that everyone knows where the latest supplier information lives.

## What Should Be Included in a Restaurant Supplier List?

A useful supplier record should contain more than a company name and phone number.

### 1. Supplier Name

Use the official supplier or company name.

If your team uses a nickname internally, keep the official name as well so invoices and orders are easy to match.

### 2. Category

Categories make long supplier lists easier to scan.

Common restaurant supplier categories include:

- produce
- meat
- seafood
- dairy
- bakery
- dry goods
- beverages
- coffee
- packaging
- cleaning products
- equipment
- maintenance

A small café may only need five or six categories.

A larger kitchen may need more.

### 3. Products Supplied

Write down the main items you normally buy from each supplier.

This does not need to be a full catalog.

For example:

**Supplier:** City Roasters  
**Products:** Espresso beans, decaf beans, filter coffee

This makes it much easier for another manager to know who to contact when something runs low.

### 4. Contact Information

Keep the contact method the team actually uses.

That might include:

- account manager
- phone
- email
- ordering portal
- WhatsApp
- emergency contact

If ordering must be made through a portal, record that too.

### 5. Order Days and Cut-Off Times

Knowing the supplier is not enough.

You also need to know **when the order must be placed**.

For example:

> Order by Monday 14:00 for Tuesday delivery.

This is especially important for kitchens that only receive certain categories a few times per week.

A missed cut-off can become an emergency purchase later.

### 6. Delivery Days

Record the normal delivery schedule.

Examples:

- Monday, Wednesday, Friday
- every Tuesday
- next-day delivery
- twice per month

If the supplier has different lead times for special products, note that too.

### 7. Minimum Order

Some suppliers require:

- a minimum order value
- a minimum number of cases
- a minimum weight
- delivery fees below a threshold

Keeping this visible helps avoid unnecessary delivery fees or rejected orders.

### 8. Payment Terms

Examples include:

- card on order
- 14-day invoice
- 30-day invoice
- direct debit
- cash on delivery

This is useful operational information, especially when more than one manager places orders.

### 9. Price History

You do not need to record every price forever.

But it is useful to know when key ingredients change significantly.

Track at least:

- current or recent price
- previous price where useful
- date of last price check

If olive oil rises from 850 SEK to 1,050 SEK per case, that is worth noticing.

### 10. Notes and Supplier Performance

A notes field can capture operational details such as:

- frequently substitutes products
- very reliable
- usually arrives before 08:00
- check invoice quantities
- requires order confirmation
- packaging quality varies
- emergency delivery available

These notes become useful when purchasing responsibility changes hands.

## Supplier List vs Product List

These are related, but they are not the same.

A **supplier list** answers:

> Who do we buy from?

A **product or inventory list** answers:

> What do we currently stock?

The two should connect.

For example:

**Inventory item:** Roma tomatoes  
**Preferred supplier:** Green Valley Produce  
**Backup supplier:** Local Market Foods  
**Par level:** 12 kg  
**Order unit:** 5 kg case

## Add a Backup Supplier for Critical Items

Not every product needs a backup supplier.

But critical items probably should.

Examples:

- cooking oil
- milk
- coffee beans
- bread
- core proteins
- takeaway packaging

A backup supplier can prevent a small delivery problem from becoming a service problem.

Add a simple field:

| Product | Preferred Supplier | Backup Supplier |
|---|---|---|
| Whole milk | DairyCo | Local Wholesale |
| Espresso beans | City Roasters | North Coffee |
| Takeaway cups | Pack & Go | Restaurant Supply AB |

Even if you rarely use the backup, having the information ready saves time.

## Track Supplier Prices Without Creating a Huge System

Restaurant teams often know that prices are rising, but not exactly where.

A lightweight price-review table can help.

| Product | Supplier | Previous Price | Current Price | Change | Last Checked |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Olive oil case | Supplier A | 850 SEK | 1,050 SEK | +200 SEK | 8 Oct |
| Chicken breast | Supplier B | 89 SEK/kg | 94 SEK/kg | +5 SEK/kg | 7 Oct |
| Coffee beans | Supplier C | 185 SEK/kg | 192 SEK/kg | +7 SEK/kg | 4 Oct |

You do not need to track every product.

Focus on:

- high-cost ingredients
- products purchased frequently
- items with volatile prices
- products that have a large impact on menu margins

A supplier list becomes much more useful when it helps the restaurant notice purchasing changes instead of just storing contact details.

## Review Suppliers Regularly

A supplier can be inexpensive and still create operational problems.

Review suppliers using practical questions.

### Reliability

- Do deliveries arrive when expected?
- Are orders complete?
- Are substitutions communicated?

### Quality

- Is the product quality consistent?
- Are damaged items common?
- Are expiration dates acceptable?

### Price

- Are prices competitive?
- Do prices change unexpectedly?
- Are delivery fees reasonable?

### Communication

- Is it easy to resolve mistakes?
- Can you reach someone when there is a problem?
- Are order confirmations clear?

### Flexibility

- Can the supplier handle emergency orders?
- Can quantities be adjusted?
- Is there a reasonable minimum order?

You do not need a complex score.

Even a simple 1–5 rating can make supplier reviews easier.

It is to make recurring supplier problems visible.

## Connect Suppliers With Low-Stock Items

Supplier management becomes more useful when it is connected to inventory.

## Check Deliveries When They Arrive

Supplier management does not end when the order is submitted.

When a delivery arrives, check:

- correct supplier
- correct items
- correct quantities
- acceptable quality
- damaged packaging
- temperatures where relevant
- substitutions
- invoice or delivery note
- missing products

If there is a problem, record it while the delivery is still fresh in everyone's memory.

This gives the restaurant useful history for future supplier reviews.

## How Supplier Problems Can Create Food Waste

Supplier management and food waste are more connected than they may appear.

Examples:

- oversized deliveries can lead to spoilage
- late deliveries can cause emergency over-ordering
- low-quality produce may be discarded early
- incorrect substitutions may go unused
- poor pack sizes can increase leftovers

## Spreadsheet vs Restaurant Supplier Management App

A spreadsheet is a good starting point.

For one location and a small supplier list, it may be enough.

But spreadsheets become harder to manage when:

- multiple staff members order
- supplier details change frequently
- inventory needs to connect to ordering
- tasks need to be assigned
- there are many recurring delivery schedules
- managers need visibility from different devices

That is when a supplier-management app becomes more useful.

The goal is not to replace a spreadsheet just because software exists.

The goal is to reduce the amount of supplier information that gets lost between stock counts, messages, invoices, and staff handovers.

## FAQ

### What is a restaurant supplier list?

A restaurant supplier list is a centralized record of the vendors a restaurant buys from. It usually includes supplier names, contacts, products, order schedules, delivery information, prices, payment terms, and operational notes.

### What should I include in a restaurant vendor list?

At minimum, include the supplier name, category, main products, contact details, order method, order day or cut-off, delivery schedule, minimum order, and useful notes.

### How often should restaurant supplier information be updated?

Update records whenever important information changes. It is also useful to review the full list every few months to remove inactive vendors, verify contacts, and check pricing or delivery changes.

### Should restaurants use more than one supplier for the same product?

Not always, but having a backup supplier for critical products can reduce disruption when the preferred supplier is out of stock, late, or unavailable.

### Is a spreadsheet enough for restaurant supplier management?

For a very small restaurant, yes. A spreadsheet can work well. A supplier-management app becomes more useful when the restaurant needs to connect suppliers with inventory, recurring tasks, multiple employees, or frequent orders.

### How does supplier management connect with inventory management?

Inventory shows what is running low. Supplier management shows where to buy it, when to order it, minimum quantities, and expected delivery timing. Connecting the two makes reordering easier and reduces emergency purchasing.

Restaurant supplier management does not need to start with complicated procurement software.
