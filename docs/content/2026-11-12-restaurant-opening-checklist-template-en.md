# Restaurant Opening Checklist Template: Get the Kitchen Ready Before Service

A restaurant can lose time before the first order is even placed.

A fridge temperature has not been recorded.

A prep station is missing an ingredient.

A delivery arrived, but nobody checked it.

The team discovers a low-stock item five minutes before service.

One person assumes somebody else turned on the equipment.

None of these problems require complicated software to prevent.

They require a repeatable opening routine.

A **restaurant opening checklist** gives the opening team a clear sequence of tasks to complete before service begins.

The goal is simple:

**the kitchen should be ready before customers create pressure.**

---

## Restaurant Opening Checklist Template

Use this as a starting point and adapt it to your restaurant, café, food truck, bakery, or catering operation.

### Shift Details

- [ ] Date
- [ ] Location
- [ ] Opening manager / shift lead
- [ ] Opening staff
- [ ] Planned service start time
- [ ] Important notes from the previous shift reviewed

### Building & Safety

- [ ] Entry and work areas inspected
- [ ] Fire exits and access routes clear
- [ ] Floors safe and free of obvious hazards
- [ ] Required ventilation/extraction running
- [ ] First-aid and safety equipment accessible
- [ ] Any overnight maintenance issue reviewed

### Equipment Start-Up

- [ ] Required cooking equipment switched on
- [ ] Coffee / beverage equipment started if applicable
- [ ] Dishwasher ready
- [ ] Refrigeration equipment operating normally
- [ ] Equipment faults reported
- [ ] Non-essential equipment remains off until needed

### Cold Storage & Temperature Checks

- [ ] Walk-in refrigerator checked
- [ ] Freezer checked
- [ ] Prep refrigerators checked
- [ ] Required temperatures recorded
- [ ] Any unusual reading escalated
- [ ] Food affected by a temperature issue reviewed before use

> Follow the food-safety requirements and temperature limits that apply in your location. This checklist is an operational template, not a substitute for local food-safety guidance.

### Hygiene & Cleaning

- [ ] Handwashing stations stocked
- [ ] Prep surfaces clean
- [ ] Sanitizing supplies ready
- [ ] Cleaning cloths/tools available
- [ ] Bins ready
- [ ] No unresolved cleaning issue from the previous shift

### Prep & Mise en Place

- [ ] Prep list reviewed
- [ ] Ingredients pulled for preparation
- [ ] Required products prepared
- [ ] Containers labelled and dated where required
- [ ] Stations stocked
- [ ] Garnishes / sauces / sides ready
- [ ] Special menu items reviewed
- [ ] Allergen-related preparation notes reviewed where relevant

### Inventory & Stock

- [ ] Critical low-stock items reviewed
- [ ] Key items physically checked
- [ ] Missing ingredients identified
- [ ] Backup products confirmed
- [ ] Items that need ordering noted
- [ ] Out-of-stock / unavailable menu items communicated

### Deliveries & Suppliers

- [ ] Expected deliveries reviewed
- [ ] Arriving products checked
- [ ] Quantities verified
- [ ] Damaged or missing products recorded
- [ ] Products stored promptly
- [ ] Supplier issues assigned for follow-up

### Staff & Service Readiness

- [ ] Scheduled opening staff present
- [ ] Roles confirmed
- [ ] Stations assigned
- [ ] Important reservations / events reviewed where relevant
- [ ] Menu changes / unavailable items communicated
- [ ] Urgent tasks assigned
- [ ] Team knows who is responsible for opening sign-off

### Final Ready-for-Service Check

- [ ] Kitchen stations ready
- [ ] Critical stock available
- [ ] Equipment operational
- [ ] Outstanding issues assigned
- [ ] Opening checklist completed
- [ ] Shift lead confirms kitchen ready for service

## Why a Dedicated Opening Checklist Matters

Your broader [Daily Kitchen Checklist Template](https://kitchflowapp.com/blog/daily-kitchen-checklist-template) can cover the whole day.

An opening checklist has a narrower job:

**make the restaurant ready before service.**

That distinction matters.

During service, teams react to customers, tickets, shortages, cleaning, waste, and changing priorities.

Before service, there is a short window where problems can be found under less pressure.

The opening checklist uses that window.

---

## 1. Review Notes From the Previous Shift

The opening shift should not begin in isolation.

Before starting prep, review any handover information such as:

- equipment problems
- products that ran out
- late deliveries
- items close to expiry
- unfinished cleaning
- supplier substitutions
- unusual waste
- maintenance issues

A short handover prevents the morning team from rediscovering yesterday's problems.

If the issue requires action, turn it into an assigned task rather than leaving it as a note.

That is where [kitchen task management](https://kitchflowapp.com/kitchen-task-management) becomes useful.

---

## 2. Start and Check Equipment Early

Equipment problems discovered during service are far more disruptive than problems discovered during opening.

Check the equipment you need before the kitchen depends on it.

This can include:

- ovens
- grills
- fryers
- extraction
- dishwashers
- coffee machines
- refrigeration
- hot holding equipment
- prep equipment

The exact list depends on the business.

The checklist should contain only the equipment the opening team actually needs to verify.

If something is not working, assign the next action immediately:

- troubleshoot
- contact maintenance
- move prep
- change menu availability
- inform the service team

---

## 3. Record Temperature Checks Properly

A checkbox that says "fridge checked" gives less information than an actual reading.

Where temperature recording is part of your operating procedure, record the value rather than only ticking the task.

Example:

| Equipment / Area | Reading | Status | Action / Note |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Walk-in refrigerator | ____ | OK / Issue | ____ |
| Freezer | ____ | OK / Issue | ____ |
| Prep refrigerator | ____ | OK / Issue | ____ |

Use the safe limits and procedures required by your local food-safety rules.

If a reading is unusual, the checklist should lead to an action—not just another tick.

---

## 4. Check the Prep List Against What Is Actually Available

A prep list can say what should be prepared.

Inventory tells you whether you actually have the ingredients.

Before beginning prep:

1. Review the prep plan.
2. Check critical ingredients.
3. Identify shortages.
4. Adjust the prep plan if necessary.
5. Communicate unavailable menu items early.

This is one reason opening tasks connect naturally with [restaurant inventory management](https://kitchflowapp.com/restaurant-inventory-management).

## 5. Use Par Levels for Opening Stock Checks

The opening team does not need to perform a complete inventory every morning.

Instead, check the products that matter most for that service.

Examples:

- milk
- coffee beans
- bread
- core proteins
- high-volume produce
- takeaway cups
- cooking oil

Compare them with a practical par or minimum level.

Example:

| Item | Opening Target | On Hand | Action |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Whole milk | 20 cartons | 13 | Refill / order |
| Espresso beans | 10 kg | 11 kg | Ready |
| Takeaway cups | 8 sleeves | 3 | Refill |
| Chicken | 12 kg | 12 kg | Ready |

The purpose is not to duplicate your full inventory process.

It is to catch problems that can affect today's service.

---

## 6. Check Expected Deliveries Before Service

If deliveries are expected in the morning, the opening checklist should include them.

Before arrival, know:

- which supplier is coming
- what is expected
- who will receive it
- where it will be stored
- whether any item is especially urgent

When the delivery arrives, check quantities and obvious issues before the products disappear into storage.

If supplier information is scattered, use a central record such as the [Restaurant Supplier List Template](https://kitchflowapp.com/blog/restaurant-supplier-list-template).

For a more connected workflow, see [restaurant supplier management](https://kitchflowapp.com/restaurant-supplier-management).

## 7. Assign Opening Tasks, Don't Just List Them

A checklist without ownership can create another problem:

everybody sees the task, but nobody knows who should do it.

Instead of:

> Check walk-in.

use:

> Walk-in temperature + low-stock check — Sofia — before 10:15.

Useful fields are:

- task
- assignee
- due time
- status
- issue / note

This becomes especially useful when several people open together.

---

## 8. Connect the Checklist to the Staff Schedule

Opening responsibilities should reflect the people actually scheduled.

The checklist should not depend on an employee who:

- is off
- starts later
- changed station
- called in sick

The schedule tells you **who is there**.

The opening checklist tells you **what the opening team needs to complete**.

See [restaurant staff scheduling](https://kitchflowapp.com/restaurant-staff-scheduling).

---

## 9. Make "Not Ready" a Valid Result

A bad checklist encourages people to tick every box.

A useful checklist allows:

- Done
- Issue
- Not applicable

If there is an issue, record:

- what happened
- who owns the follow-up
- whether it affects service

Example:

| Opening Task | Status | Issue | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| Check prep fridge | Issue | Not cooling normally | Manager |
| Verify tomato stock | Issue | Only 2 kg available | Purchasing |
| Start dishwasher | Done | — | — |

A checklist should reveal problems, not hide them.

---

## 10. Keep Deep Cleaning Out of the Daily Opening List

A common checklist mistake is putting everything into one list.

Daily opening should focus on **service readiness**.

Deep-cleaning tasks such as:

- extraction-filter cleaning
- detailed refrigerator cleaning
- storage reorganization
- equipment deep clean

may belong on a weekly schedule instead.

Keeping the opening checklist focused makes it more likely the team will actually finish it before service.

## Paper Opening Checklist vs Opening Checklist App

Paper can work very well for one small team.

It is visible and requires almost no setup.

But paper becomes harder to manage when you need:

- recurring opening tasks
- different assignees
- completion history
- remote manager visibility
- several shifts
- several locations
- unresolved issues to carry forward

At that point, a checklist app can make the routine easier to manage.

The goal is not to digitize a checklist just because you can.

The goal is to make sure the opening routine actually happens and problems are followed up.

## Opening Checklist: Keep These 5 Rules

## 1. Keep it specific

"Prepare kitchen" is not a useful task.

"Check walk-in temperature" is.

## 2. Assign responsibility

Important opening tasks should have an owner.

## 3. Use actual readings where useful

For things like temperature checks, a recorded value provides more information than a tick.

## 4. Record problems

A checklist should show what failed, not encourage people to mark everything complete.

## 5. Review it regularly

Remove tasks nobody needs and add tasks when recurring problems appear.

## FAQ

## What should be included in a restaurant opening checklist?

Typical opening tasks include building and safety checks, equipment start-up, temperature records, hygiene checks, prep and mise en place, critical inventory, expected deliveries, staff assignments, and a final ready-for-service check.

## Who should complete the restaurant opening checklist?

That depends on the team. A shift lead may own the checklist while individual tasks are assigned to cooks, servers, baristas, or other opening staff.

## How long should a restaurant opening checklist be?

It should be short enough to complete before service while covering the tasks that genuinely affect readiness, safety, stock, equipment, and the team. Move non-essential deep-cleaning work to separate recurring checklists.

## Is an opening checklist the same as a daily kitchen checklist?

No. An opening checklist focuses specifically on preparing the restaurant before service. A daily checklist can also include tasks during service and at closing.

## Should stock checks be part of the opening checklist?

Critical stock should usually be checked before service so shortages can be handled early. A complete inventory count can remain a separate scheduled process.

## Conclusion

A restaurant opening checklist should answer one question:

**Are we actually ready for service?**

Before customers arrive, the team should know:

- equipment is working
- required checks are complete
- prep is ready
- critical stock is available
- deliveries are understood
- staff know their roles
- unresolved issues have an owner

Keep the list practical.

Assign responsibility.

Record problems instead of hiding them.

And keep the opening routine separate from the longer list of things the restaurant needs to do throughout the day.
